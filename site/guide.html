A group of panels

A <bento-group> lays out its panels in a row. Panels without a size share the space equally. Put a <bento-separator> between two panels to let the user drag the line between them.

Two
Three

One panel always stays flexible, so the row is always full. Dragging between two flexible panels gives the earlier one a size, and the later one keeps filling. With no flexible panel at all, the last one fills.

A separator resizes and collapses its primary panel: of its two neighbours, the one with a size or collapsible, the later one when both or neither have one, or the one its aria-controls names. It has no attributes of its own. Separators are yours to write; bento never inserts one, so it cannot fight a framework that owns the DOM.

Size, min and max

size is where a panel starts, min and max bound what the user can drag it to. Lengths are pixels or percentages of the group's space; any other unit falls back to the default. Dragging always writes pixels.

A panel clips its content, and the content keeps at least min, so it is cut off rather than squished. DOM order is priority: earlier panels keep their space. Dragging pushes the panels further along down to their min, and when the group itself gets too small, it collapses collapsible panels from the end and expands them once the space returns.

Collapsing and snapping

A collapsible panel can collapse to its collapsed-size, 0 by default. Drag it below its min and it holds there; drag past halfway to its collapsed size and it snaps shut. Drag back past halfway and it opens again. Press Enter on the separator to collapse or expand it. Expanding restores the size it had before.

The buttons write the panel's collapsed property, which animates like a toggle. Collapsing and snapping animate; a plain drag, the first layout and a window resize never do. A panel collapsed to 0 hides its content with display: none, so focus and assistive technology skip it. The separator stays next to it, so it can open it again.

Arrow keys snap as soon as they go below min, so a keyboard user never gets stuck at the hold.

Why halfway: snap at halfway. How it animates: Web Animations. Under reduced motion: a cross-fade.

Keyboard

Every separator is focusable. It adds tabindex="0" when you did not write one. Keys act on its primary panel:

Key Does
Left and Right arrows, or Up and Down in a vertical group Move the separator 10 pixels; with Shift, 100 pixels.
Enter Collapse or expand the primary panel, when it is collapsible.
Home Move the primary panel to its min.
End Move the primary panel to its max, or as far as the others allow.
Double-click Reset the primary panel to its default size and collapsed state.

Adding and removing panels

Panels may appear, disappear or collapse at any moment, even in the middle of a drag. The group lays itself out again whenever its children change.

Modal panels

On a small screen there is no room for sidebars. Give a panel a modal media query, and while it matches, the panel leaves the group and shows as a modal dialog over it, while it is not collapsed. Entering modal mode collapses it, so it arrives closed. Leaving restores the collapsed state it had beside the content.

Drag the divider to make the frame below narrower than 480 pixels. Its filters panel becomes a drawer, and a button appears to open it. Close it with Escape, the Close button, or a tap outside.

The page inside the frame, and its styles:

  • Opening is yours. Write collapsed = false, from a button. Only closing can come from the user.
  • Closing is collapsing. Escape, the back gesture and a tap outside collapse the panel, so they fire a cancelable beforetoggle, and your app can keep the drawer open. Why there is no open attribute
  • One at a time. Showing one modal panel collapses any other, like popover="auto".
  • Style the panel itself. By default the sheet is full height, on the side of the group the panel sits on, as wide as its size and never wider than the viewport. In modal mode, the panel's size, padding, background, border, radius and shadow go to the sheet, so a bottom sheet or a full screen is plain CSS on the panel. Why no part is exposed
  • The backdrop takes its color from --bento-backdrop on the panel.
  • Name it. The panel's aria-label names the dialog.
  • Menus render inside. The rest of the page is inert while a modal panel shows, so a menu opened in it must render inside it.
  • The separator next to a modal panel hides.

This site does the same: make the window narrower than 1100 pixels and the inspector becomes a drawer, narrower than 720 and the navigation does too.

Events

Events fire on the panel and never bubble. To hear them on a parent, listen in the capture phase. None fires when your code writes a property, and none for the first layout.

Event Fires Cancelable
resize When the user changes the panel's size, directly or by pushing it, at most once per frame. No
resizeend When the drag or key press is done, on pointer or key up, like scrollend. No
beforetoggle Before the panel collapses or expands, for every change you did not write yourself. Only when the user caused it
toggle After it collapsed or expanded. No

beforetoggle and toggle are ToggleEvents whose oldState and newState are "open" (expanded) or "closed" (collapsed). The user causes a toggle with Enter, a drag that snaps, a double-click, or by closing a modal panel. A group collapsing panels as it shrinks, a panel entering modal mode, or one modal panel closing another fire the same pair, but beforetoggle cannot be canceled then: the space is gone.

    Live state and defaults

    size and collapsed work like value and checked on an <input>. The attribute is the default, reflected by defaultSize and defaultCollapsed. The property is the live state: dragging, collapsing and writing it change it, and the attribute stays as written. While nobody has changed the live state, a changed attribute applies to it, without animating. Every other property simply reflects its attribute.

    Writing collapsed animates like a toggle and fires no event, because your code knows what it wrote. That makes buttons simple, like the ones in this site's title bar:

    Styling

    bento's own styles are barebones, like the browser's for an <input>, and live inside the elements as :host rules. Any rule of yours wins, Tailwind classes included, whatever their layer or specificity. They use system colors, so dark mode and forced colors work before you style anything.

    Put one element in each panel

    Your content sits in an internal wrapper that clips it. Put one element in the panel that carries layout, padding and scrolling, such as h-full overflow-auto in Tailwind, or the .tray in these demos. Padding and borders on the sides that collapse go on that element, not the panel, or the panel stops collapsing at its padding. Margins on panels and separators are not supported; gap on the group is.

    Layout is bento's: a flex or flex-basis of yours on a panel overrides it.

    Toggle timing and live state

    Collapsing animates with the panel's own transition-duration and transition-timing-function, 200 milliseconds ease by default. So duration-700 ease-out slows it down. Any other transition on a panel would animate drags too. Live state is a custom state: :state(collapsed) and :state(modal) on a panel, :state(dragging) on a separator. The attributes are readable as --bento-size, --bento-min, --bento-max and --bento-collapsed-size.
