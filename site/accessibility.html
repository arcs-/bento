Accessibility

Separators follow the WAI-ARIA window splitter pattern, including its optional keys where they fit. What bento cannot know, the names, stays with you.

What you write

  • A label on every separator. An aria-label, or aria-labelledby pointing at its panel's visible title. bento cannot know a good label.
  • aria-controls with the primary panel's id, for browsers without ARIA element reflection. Where reflection exists, bento points the separator at its primary panel itself; your attribute also picks the primary panel.
  • An aria-label on a modal panel. It names the dialog.
  • tabindex="0" on separators when a framework hydrates them, so the server's markup matches. Elsewhere bento adds it.

What bento does

  • The separator has the role separator, an orientation, and aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin and aria-valuemax: the primary panel's share of the group, 0 to 100. All of it goes through ElementInternals, so none of it appears as an attribute in your DOM or fights your framework. Why
  • The separator itself is focusable, so it carries your label. Arrow keys resize by 10 pixels, 100 with Shift; Enter collapses; Home and End go to min and max. Keyboard
  • Arrow keys snap a collapsible panel as soon as they go below min, so the drag's halfway threshold never stops a keyboard user. Why
  • A panel collapsed to 0 hides its content with display: none: focus and assistive technology skip it. A rail keeps its content.
  • A modal panel is a real modal <dialog>: it sits in the top layer, the rest of the page is inert, and Escape and the back gesture close it.
  • F6, the pattern's key for cycling between panes, is left to your app. It is a page-wide shortcut, and only the app knows its landmarks.

Motion

Collapsing and snapping animate, so you can see what moved. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, the size changes at once and the content cross-fades instead: out, then the jump, when collapsing; the jump, then in, when expanding. Dragging is never animated; the content follows the pointer, which is direct manipulation. Why a fade rather than nothing

Color and touch

bento's default styles use system colors, so dark mode and forced colors work before you style anything. The separator is a 1 pixel line whose hit area reaches 12 pixels to each side, 20 on touch screens.
